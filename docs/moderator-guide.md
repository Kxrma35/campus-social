# Moderator guide

For the people who review reports in the Moku moderator console. Read it once before your first shift. It is a draft: the rules of the community (`legal/COMMUNITY_RULES.md`) are drafts too, and a lawyer has not reviewed either.

## What you are for

Moku is a social app for verified USIU-Africa students, aged 18 and over. Students can post under their own name or anonymously. Your job is to look at what students have reported and decide, quickly and fairly, whether it breaks the community rules, and to act in a way that protects people without punishing them more than the facts justify.

You are **not** a police officer, a counsellor or a judge. You apply a short set of rules. When something is beyond that, you escalate it to the incident lead (see the end).

## The three promises you keep

1. **Anonymity.** You never try to find out who is behind an anonymous profile, and you never help anyone else do it. The console does not show it, and a penalty on an anonymous profile lands on the account behind it without telling you whose. If someone asks you to "just check" or guesses aloud, say no and tell the incident lead. Identity lookups are a separate, audited process for named trust and safety officers under written rules; it is not part of your role.
2. **Reporters stay hidden.** You cannot see, and must never speculate about, who reported something. Never tell a student that someone reported them, even by hinting.
3. **Confidentiality.** What you see in the console stays in the console. Do not take screenshots or copy content to chat, email or a personal phone. Do not talk about reports with friends, even if the people involved are known to you.

## Access

- You sign in at the moderator console with a **personal token** given to you privately. Do not share it, write it in a message, or reuse one from someone else. If you lose it or think someone else has seen it, tell the operator at once so it can be revoked and replaced.
- The console is reachable **only privately** (an SSH tunnel or VPN). It is never on the open internet. If you can reach it from a public network, tell the operator.
- Sign out when you leave your seat. The token is kept only for that browser tab.

## The queue

- Reports are grouped by what was reported: a post, a comment or a profile.
- **Urgent items come first** and are marked in words. Urgent means a threat, a possible self-harm risk or an intimate image shared without consent. The target is **1 hour**. Everything else is **24 hours**.
- Each item shows a due time and whether it is overdue. Nothing sends you an alert: **you have to look.**
- Posts and comments that match a crisis phrase are put in the queue automatically as urgent. The phrase list is crude: it misses a lot and flags quotes and song lyrics, so treat an automatic flag as a prompt to read, not as proof.

## What the reasons mean

| Reason | Typical content | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Threat | A threat of violence, or someone in danger | Urgent. Consider the incident lead and, if there is a real risk, the university's safety routes. |
| Self-harm | Someone may be thinking of harming themselves | Urgent. The author already sees the support information. Do not punish a person for being in distress. |
| Intimate image | An intimate image shared without consent | Urgent. Hide it first, then decide. Do not forward or save it. |
| Harassment | Bullying, targeting a named person | Look at the pattern, not one comment. |
| Private info | Someone's phone, address, photos, or other private details | Hide first; consider whether it identifies a person. |
| Hate | Hate against a group | |
| Impersonation | Pretending to be someone else | Includes a profile photo of another person. |
| Other | Anything else | Decide whether a rule is broken at all. |

## Opening an item

You see the content, any photos with the author's own description, every report with its reason and detail, and what has already been done. Read the whole thing before you decide. Look at the context: a reply to a joke reads differently from a stand-alone post. If you know the people involved, **step away and pass it to someone else**. A moderator who cannot be fair, or cannot be seen to be, should not decide it.

## What you can do

| Action | What it does | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Dismiss** the reports | Closes them and shows the content again if it was hidden only because of report volume | The content does not break a rule |
| **Uphold** the reports | Hides the content and closes the reports as actioned | The content breaks a rule |
| **Remove a profile photo** (on a profile report) | The photo stops showing and is deleted soon after. The account is untouched. Needs a written reason | The photo breaks a rule, but the person does not need a penalty |
| **Warning** | Recorded against the account; counts towards locking anonymous posting | A first or minor breach |
| **Suspension** (1 hour to 30 days) | The person cannot post for that time; they are signed out | A repeated or more serious breach |
| **Ban** | Closes the account, signs it out, hides its posts and comments, and prevents signing up again with that email. The student can appeal once | Serious harm, or repeated serious breaches |

**Every penalty needs a written reason of at least 10 characters.** Write it so another moderator, or the student in an appeal, can see why. Facts, not feelings: "Posted a named classmate's phone number after being warned twice" is a reason; "Awful person" is not.

**Always take the smallest action that fixes the harm.** Hiding a post and a warning is often enough. A ban is the strongest thing available and is reversible only through an appeal.

Every action asks you to confirm and says plainly what it will do. Read it.

## Anonymous authors

You will see "Anonymous profile" and a generated handle. Treat them exactly as you would a known student: the same rules, the same bar for evidence. Do not infer who it is from the writing, the time, a photo or a joke only certain people would make. If a penalty is applied, the console says it applies to the verified account behind the profile and does not tell you who.

## Appeals

A banned student gets one appeal. You see their message and decide: **upheld** (the account, profiles and content the ban hid are restored) or **denied**. A decision needs a written reason. Look at the facts afresh, not at whether the first moderator was "right". If the first decision was yours, ask a colleague to decide.

## Things you must never do

- Try to learn who owns an anonymous profile, or who made a report.
- Take, share or describe content outside the console.
- Act on a report about someone you know, or a dispute you are part of.
- Contact the student through any other channel, or make a deal ("delete it and I'll drop it").
- Delete something to avoid trouble, or change the facts in a reason.
- Decide when you are exhausted or upset. Hand over.

## Looking after yourself

You will see unpleasant content. You may stop at any time and hand an item to someone else without explaining. Do not carry a shift alone through a run of distressing reports. Tell the incident lead if you are struggling; the operator should agree who covers and who you can talk to **before** the first shift.

## Escalate to the incident lead straight away when

- there is a real threat to someone's safety;
- you think the console, a token or the system has been misused or seen by someone it should not have;
- someone asks you to identify an anonymous author or a reporter;
- a report involves staff, a lecturer or a person of authority;
- the queue is overdue and you cannot clear it. Say so; do not quietly miss the target.

See `ops/BREACH_PROCEDURE.md` for what the incident lead does.

## Open decisions the operator must make before you start

- Who covers nights, weekends and exam weeks, and who is the deputy (open decision 6).
- Who the incident lead is and how to reach them at 02:00.
- The university's own routes for threats and welfare concerns, and how to reach them.
- Whether moderators are staff or trained students, and what training they get.

None of these is decided yet.
