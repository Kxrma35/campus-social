# USIU-Africa Student Interview Guide

Status: draft, 2026-10-12. Purpose: validate the problem before building much (build plan milestone M6).

## What we need to learn

1. Do students hold back on existing platforms because of audience (family, relatives, family friends)?
2. Would they join a student-only app, and would they post, not just read?
3. Which profile would they use more: known or anonymous?
4. What would make them trust it, and what would make them leave?

Avoid pitching. We are testing the problem, so do not describe the solution until the final section.

## Setup

- 6 to 10 interviews, 25 to 35 minutes each, mix of year groups, courses, and heavy and light social media users.
- Only interview students who are 18 or older.
- Recruit by direct invitation (clubs, classes, friends of friends). Do not recruit only from your own circle.
- Offer a small thank-you only if it is the same for everyone; do not tie it to answers.
- Two people if possible: one asks, one takes notes.
- Do not record audio or video unless the participant agrees on the consent script below.

## Consent script (read aloud before starting)

> Thanks for talking with me. I'm researching how students use social media, for a project I'm working on. It takes about 30 minutes.
>
> - Taking part is voluntary. You can skip any question or stop at any time.
> - I'll write notes. I won't write down your name next to your answers, and I won't share your answers in a way that identifies you.
> - Would you be okay with me recording audio so I don't miss anything? You can say no and I'll just take notes. If you say yes, I'll delete the recording after I've written up the notes.
> - You don't need to share anything about specific people or anything you're not comfortable with.
>
> Do you have any questions? Are you happy to continue?

Record only: consent given (yes/no), recording consent (yes/no), year of study, and course area. No names, phone numbers or emails in the notes.

## Questions

### A. Warm-up (3 min)
1. Which social apps do you use most? Roughly how often?
2. Which do you post on, and which do you mostly just scroll?

### B. Current behaviour (8 to 10 min)
3. Tell me about the last thing you wanted to post but didn't. What stopped you?
4. Who do you think of when you decide whether to post something? (Probe: parents, relatives, family friends, lecturers, future employers, classmates.)
5. Do you have a second or private account anywhere? What do you use it for? Who knows about it?
6. Have you ever deleted something after posting? What happened?
7. Is there anything you only share with friends in person or in a group chat, but not on a public feed? Why there and not elsewhere?

### C. Pain and workarounds (5 min)
8. What do you do today when you want to say something to other students but not to family?
9. What works about that? What is annoying or risky about it?
10. Have you seen anonymous or student-only spaces before (confession pages, group chats, other apps)? What was good or bad about them?

### D. Safety and trust (5 to 7 min)
11. What would worry you about a student-only app where some people post anonymously?
12. What would you want to be true before you'd trust it?
13. Who should be able to find out who is behind an anonymous profile, and in what situations?
14. How should rule-breaking be handled? Who should decide?

### E. Concept reaction (5 min, only now)

Describe in two or three sentences: *"An app only verified students at your campus can see. You have a normal profile, and you can add a separate anonymous one. You choose, post by post, which one to speak as."*

15. In your own words, what is this?
16. Would you join? What would you expect to see there on day one?
17. Which profile would you use more? Why?
18. What would you post, and what would you never post, in each?
19. What would make you stop using it?
20. What worries you most about it?

### F. Close (2 min)
21. Is there anything I should have asked and didn't?
22. Would you be open to testing an early version later? (Collect contact only if they say yes, kept separately from their answers.)

## Probes to use throughout
- "Can you give me an example?"
- "What happened next?"
- "Why does that matter to you?"
- "Is that something you've actually done, or something you imagine you'd do?"

## Pitfalls to avoid
- Do not lead ("Wouldn't it be great if...").
- Do not promise secrecy the app cannot deliver; if asked, say anonymity is from other students, not from the company, and that screenshots can't be prevented.
- Do not push for details about named individuals.
- If a student describes harm to themselves or others, stop the interview, be kind, and share USIU counselling services. Confirm the helpline details before fieldwork starts.

## Synthesis

After 6 to 10 interviews, tally:
- How many held back posting because of audience.
- How many would join, and how many would post (not just read).
- Known vs anonymous preference split.
- Top 3 trust conditions and top 3 worries.

Signal that the idea is worth building: a clear majority describe audience-driven self-censorship unprompted (section B), and several say they would post, not only read.
